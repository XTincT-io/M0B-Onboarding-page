Transmission 02

Blockchain, in plain terms

Four concepts cover almost everything you need before your first mint. No jargon beyond this.

01 / LEDGER

What a blockchain is

A public record shared across thousands of computers instead of one company's server. Once something is written to it, it can't be quietly edited — that's what makes ownership on it mean something.

02 / NFT

What "minting" means

Minting writes a unique, traceable record of your track (or art tied to it) onto the chain, with your wallet as the origin. The NFT is the certificate of ownership — the audio file itself is usually stored alongside it, not "inside" it.

03 / GAS

Why transactions cost money

"Gas" is the small fee paid to the network for processing your transaction. It varies by chain and congestion — some chains (Tezos, Polygon, XRPL) are built to keep this near-zero, which matters for artists minting often.

04 / CHAINS

The landscape you're choosing from

EVM chains (Ethereum, Base, Polygon) dominate NFT tooling. Tezos and Cardano built early artist-first, low-fee ecosystems. XRPL and Polkadot are newer entrants for music-specific rails. Bitcoin now supports NFTs via Ordinals.

Transmission 03

Get a wallet per chain

A wallet is your identity and your signature on-chain — it's what you'll use to mint, receive, and sell. You don't need all of these; only set up a wallet for the chain(s) you plan to release on.

EVM

Ethereum / Base / Polygon

MetaMask, Rainbow, or Coinbase Wallet. The most widely supported across NFT marketplaces.

POLKADOT

Polkadot / parachains

Polkadot{.js}, Talisman, or SubWallet.

TEZOS

Tezos (XTZ)

Temple Wallet or Kukai — the standard pair for Tezos-based NFT platforms.

CARDANO

Cardano (ADA)

Eternl, Yoroi, or Lace.

XRPL

XRP Ledger

Xaman (formerly Xumm) — the default wallet for XRPL-native NFTs.

BITCOIN

Bitcoin (Ordinals)

Sparrow or Xverse for ordinals, or a Ledger/Trezor hardware wallet for cold storage.

Transmission 04 — Priority

Wallet safety is non-negotiable

Most people don't lose funds to hacks — they lose them to phishing links and shared seed phrases. Five rules cover almost every real-world loss.

Your seed phrase is the wallet

Anyone with your 12–24 word recovery phrase has full control of everything in that wallet. No legitimate platform, marketplace, or support agent will ever ask for it — not in a DM, not on a call.

Write it down, offline

Paper or a metal backup, stored somewhere physical. Never a screenshot, note app, email draft, or cloud file.

Verify every URL before connecting

Fake marketplace and mint-site clones are the most common attack. Bookmark real links, and double-check before approving any wallet connection or transaction.

Use a hardware wallet for anything valuable

A Ledger or Trezor keeps your keys offline. For a wallet holding real earnings or masters, this is the single biggest upgrade you can make.

Separate "hot" and "cold" wallets

Keep a small working wallet for day-to-day minting and connecting to sites. Keep a separate wallet — ideally hardware-backed — for anything you're not actively using.
